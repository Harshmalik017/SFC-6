# Information Being Collected from Local Bodies through SFC Portal and Its Relevance for the Recommendations of the Sixth State Finance Commission

## 1. Background and Purpose of Information Collection

The Sixth State Finance Commission has asked different tiers of local bodies to submit information through the SFC portal using their respective login IDs. The objective is to create a structured and comparable database for Urban Local Bodies, District Panchayats, Kshetra Panchayats and Gram Panchayats. The information being collected covers demographic profile, area, administrative structure, financial receipts, expenditure, infrastructure, services, audit position, liabilities and utilisation of grants. This will help the Commission understand the real financial position and functional needs of each tier before making its recommendations.

## 2. Basic Profile of Urban Local Bodies

For Urban Local Bodies, the format asks for the name of the district, type of body—Nagar Panchayat, Nagar Palika Parishad or Nagar Nigam—and the name of the local body. It also seeks the year of constitution, population as per Census 2011, social category-wise population, current area in square kilometres, year of last boundary revision, number of wards, number and area of slums, and schemes for self-employment of the urban poor. These details will help the Commission classify urban bodies according to size, population pressure, administrative spread and socio-economic burden.

## 3. Audit, Liabilities and Institutional Position of Urban Bodies

The urban format also asks for audit-related information for the years 2020-21 to 2024-25. It seeks details of the years for which audit has been completed, the years for which audit objections have been complied with or settled, and the years for which audit certificates have been issued by the Director, Local Fund Audit. It also asks for loans taken by the body and outstanding repayment liability, along with institutions run by the body such as hospitals and schools/colleges. This information will help the Commission judge financial discipline, accounting reliability, pending liabilities and the institutional responsibilities of each urban body.

## 4. Taxes, Fees and User Charges of Urban Bodies

A detailed part of the urban format relates to taxes, fees and licence fees imposed by the local body. It asks whether property tax, water tax, sewer tax, drainage tax, tax on transfer of immovable property, vacant land tax, tax on trades and professions, advertisement tax, show tax, betterment tax, parking tax, mutation fee, birth/death certificate fee, licence fee, copy fee and other charges are imposed. It also asks for the rates applicable in 2020-21, the financial year in which the rates were last determined, and the rates currently in force. This will enable the Commission to assess the own-source revenue potential of urban bodies and whether they are updating tax and fee rates properly.

## 5. Income and Receipts of Urban Bodies

The Commission has asked urban bodies to provide details of total income from different sources. These include income from taxes and fees imposed by the body, funds received on the basis of State Finance Commission recommendations, grants received under Central Finance Commission recommendations, State Government grants, Central Government grants, grants from domestic or international institutions/persons, Uttar Pradesh Trade Development Fund, other grants and other sources. This will help the Commission distinguish between own revenue, assigned/devolved funds and scheme-based grants. It will also show how dependent each body is on transfers from the State or Centre.

## 6. Expenditure and Service Delivery by Urban Bodies

The urban format seeks expenditure details against various receipts and also sector-wise information relating to municipal services. The information includes water supply source, quantity of water produced, number of pumps/tubewells, household and non-household water connections, prevailing water rates, sale rate of water and per capita daily availability of drinking water. Similar information is sought in relation to sanitation, roads, drainage, street lights and other civic services. This will help the Commission compare expenditure with actual service delivery and identify service gaps which may require grants or reform-linked support.

## 7. Basic Profile of Panchayats

For Panchayati Raj Institutions, the format asks for the type of Panchayat—District Panchayat, Kshetra Panchayat or Gram Panchayat—along with the name of the district and Panchayat. It seeks general information such as population as per Census 2011, male-female population, Scheduled Caste population, Scheduled Tribe population, Other Backward Class population, number of elected members, name of the Chairperson/Pramukh/Pradhan and other basic details. This will help the Commission understand the population base, representation structure and social composition of rural local bodies.

## 8. Area, Households and Rural Socio-Economic Information

The Panchayat format also asks for total area, agricultural land, inhabited and uninhabited area, number of households, BPL households, category-wise BPL households, number of revenue villages, number of Gram Panchayats under a Kshetra Panchayat or District Panchayat, year of formation and year of last boundary revision. These details are important because the expenditure needs of a Panchayat depend not only on population but also on geographical spread, number of villages, poverty burden and area to be serviced.

## 9. Infrastructure and Economic Environment of Panchayats

The Commission has asked Panchayats to provide information on Panchayat Bhawan, Gram Panchayat Secretariat, community halls, buildings owned by Panchayats, private buildings/land used, shops, mobile towers, haat/bazaar, farm houses and banquet halls. The format also asks about educational institutions, medical and health infrastructure, animal husbandry facilities, roads, public toilets, industries, mining areas, drinking water facilities and street lighting. This information will help the Commission assess the local infrastructure base, revenue-generating assets and the gap in basic services.

## 10. Audit, Loans and Staff Position of Panchayats

For Panchayats, the format seeks details of audit completion, settlement of audit objections, audit certificates issued, loans taken and repayment liability. It also asks for information about sanctioned posts, working employees, salary and allowances expenditure, third-class employees, fourth-class employees and contractual/daily wage workers. This will help the Commission understand whether Panchayats have adequate manpower, whether staff costs are reasonable, and whether financial records are reliable enough for grant-based recommendations.

## 11. Receipts and Expenditure of Panchayats

The Panchayat formats seek information on past income, including funds received under State Finance Commission recommendations, interest on deposits, contributions received from District Panchayats to Gram Panchayats, grants from national/international institutions, CSR and other sources. They also ask for utilisation of funds received under SFC recommendations, including expenditure on street lights, handpump repair, maintenance of assets covering more than one Gram Panchayat, public distribution-related infrastructure, honorarium, meetings, travel allowance and other works. This will help the Commission examine whether previous grants were actually used for local development and maintenance purposes.

## 12. Specific Information from Kshetra Panchayats

For Kshetra Panchayats, the format separately asks for population, SC/ST/OBC population, elected members, number of wards, number of Gram Panchayats under the Kshetra Panchayat, year of boundary revision and audit status for the years 2020-21 to 2024-25. It further seeks receipts from State Finance Commission, Central Finance Commission, State Government, awards, CSR/donation and own sources, along with expenditure against these receipts. This will help the Commission evaluate the intermediate-tier role of Kshetra Panchayats and their actual financial and developmental relevance.

## 13. Information on Government Schemes and Fund Utilisation

The Kshetra Panchayat format asks for funds received and utilised under various government schemes such as MGNREGA, Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana, rural drinking water schemes, Swachh Bharat Mission, Mid-Day Meal, pensions, scholarships, NRHM, Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan, multi-sectoral plans and other schemes. It also asks for works carried out from State Finance Commission funds, including operation of primary health centres, veterinary hospitals, seed centres, implementation and monitoring of rural development programmes and works covering more than one Gram Panchayat. This will help the Commission understand the extent to which different tiers are handling scheme funds and whether SFC grants are supplementing or duplicating other government schemes.

## 14. Usefulness for Vertical Devolution

The data collected through the portal will help the Commission decide the overall share of State resources that should be devolved to local bodies. By comparing population, area, service responsibilities, existing revenue, expenditure, infrastructure gaps and liabilities, the Commission can assess the total financial requirement of local bodies. This will be useful for recommending how much financial support should flow from the State to Urban Local Bodies and Panchayati Raj Institutions as a whole.

## 15. Usefulness for Horizontal Distribution

The information will also help in deciding how funds should be distributed among different local bodies of the same tier. Bodies with larger population, wider area, weaker own-source revenue, higher backwardness, higher infrastructure gap or greater service burden may require higher support. At the same time, audit compliance, utilisation of previous grants, own revenue effort and maintenance of records may be used to design performance-based or reform-linked incentives.

## 16. Usefulness for Sector-Specific Recommendations

Since the formats collect sector-wise information on water supply, sanitation, roads, lighting, health, education, buildings, markets and other infrastructure, the Commission can identify sectors where the gap is more serious. This can support recommendations for specific-purpose grants, maintenance grants, service-level improvement grants or conditional grants for priority sectors.

## 17. Usefulness for Governance and Reform Recommendations

The audit, staffing, liability, tax-rate and utilisation-related information will help the Commission identify governance weaknesses. For example, if a body has not updated user charges, has poor audit compliance, has large outstanding liabilities or is not utilising grants effectively, the Commission can recommend reforms relating to accounting, audit, revenue mobilisation, staffing pattern, online reporting and monitoring.

## 18. Overall Conclusion

Thus, the portal-based information being collected from different tiers of local bodies will provide an evidence-based foundation for the Sixth State Finance Commission's recommendations. It will help the Commission move beyond general assumptions and make recommendations based on actual data relating to need, capacity, performance and service delivery. The exercise will therefore support a more transparent, objective and need-based devolution formula for local bodies.
